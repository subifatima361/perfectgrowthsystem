01 · Who she is and who she sells to

The niche

Lacey Newman is a business strategist and coach for high-achieving women entrepreneurs. Her line is "I help high-achieving women rise with aligned power, peace, and profit." She sells clarity, structure and self-leadership to women who built their business by doing everything themselves and are now stretched thin.

Her offers:

  • Rise Higher Society™, an 8-week live group coaching programme on strategy, systems and self-leadership, then how to amplify it with AI.
  • Free strategy calls ("Lets Talk Strategy", "Schedule a Call").
  • The free calendar (this campaign). It's the top of her funnel and feeds the calls and the Society.

The audience in these ads: women founders, often moms, running service businesses where the business "runs through them". More clients means more of their own hours. They're successful, not struggling. Their problem is time and capacity, not getting started.

Her voice: warm, calm, a little elevated, like a friend inviting you in. Her buttons say "Pull Up a Chair" and "Drop Me a Note". The ads should feel like a quiet exhale in the feed, not hype. Scroll-stoppers get attention with an idea, never with shouting or urgency tricks.

Tone guardrail: no fake scarcity, no income claims, no before/after. Meta also restricts ads that state personal attributes about the viewer. Audience call-outs like "For women founders" are fine; avoid lines like "You're a burnt-out mom".

Photography and styling

  • Only her own photos. Her site has a branded shoot: Lacey on a rust velvet chair, at a white kitchen counter, sitting against a sunlit white wall, plus flat-lays (laptop on rattan, spiral planner, pink teapot and journal, incense by a laptop). The mock-ups below use crops from your screenshots; pull full-resolution versions from the site or ask her for the shoot folder.
  • Light: soft natural daylight, warm whites, real shadows. Never flash, never saturated.
  • Graphics: where there's no photo, build the visual from type, the glow circle or a simple UI mock-up (phone, inbox, calendar). No illustration packs, no 3D, no emoji.
  • Buttons: two styles from her site. A solid terracotta block with a white serif label and square corners, and a serif italic text link with an underline ("Join Now", "Schedule a Call"). No rounded pills.
  • Layout: generous margins, left-aligned or centred editorial blocks, one accent colour per ad. Her section numbers ("1." in terracotta serif) are a nice detail to borrow.
  • Italic for emphasis: set one or two key words of a headline in the italic, recoloured sand (on teal) or terracotta (on light).

07 · Before the designer starts

Open items

  • Confirm the display serif's name on laceynewman.com (WhatFont), and get the font file or licence so the ads match the site exactly.
  • Get full-resolution photos from Lacey's brand shoot. The mock-ups use low-resolution crops from screenshots.
  • Check the landing page (go.laceynewman.com/reclaim-15-hours) matches the ads: same "Reclaim 15 Hours" name, same colours, and a calendar preview image if possible. The page couldn't be loaded from my side.
  • Test order: launch 2A (badge), 1C (lock screen) and 3B (≠) first; they're the fastest to read. Then rotate in the rest.
  • Mock-up type is a stand-in. Line breaks and sizes will shift slightly once the real display font is in.
